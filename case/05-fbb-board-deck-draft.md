## Slide 5: Two documents set the bar: BCBS 239 and the ECB's 2024 guide

**BCBS 239 (January 2013):** Basel Committee principles for effective risk data aggregation and risk reporting. 14 principles in four groups: governance and infrastructure, aggregation, reporting, supervisory review. Principles 1–11 apply to banks.
**ECB guide on RDARR (May 2024):** sets out the ECB's supervisory expectations in seven areas, from the management body's responsibilities to implementation programmes. To be read together with BCBS 239. RDARR is an ECB supervisory priority for 2024–2026.

*Source: BCBS, Principles for effective risk data aggregation and risk reporting (2013); ECB, Guide on effective risk data aggregation and risk reporting (May 2024)*

---

## Slide 6: Most European banks are still struggling to meet BCBS 239

**72%** of ECB-supervised banks failed their most recent BCBS 239 assessment

- Supervisors have lost patience after more than ten years
- The ECB expects significant institutions to make substantial progress
- Weak data governance and fragmented IT are the most common root causes
- Banks that move early gain credibility with their supervisor

*Source: ECB Supervisory Newsletter, Q2 2025; ECB guide on RDARR (2024)*

---

## Slide 7: Five BCBS 239 principles matter most for FBB

- **Principle 1 · Governance:** Framework approved by board and senior management; independent validation
- **Principle 2 · Data architecture:** Integrated data taxonomies and architecture across the banking group
- **Principle 3 · Accuracy and integrity:** Risk data reconciled with sources, including accounting data
- **Principle 6 · Adaptability:** Meet ad hoc data requests quickly, especially in stress
- **Principle 17 · Data lineage:** End-to-end lineage for every critical data element

*Source: BCBS 239 (2013)*

---

## Slide 8: Ownership of risk data is clear for credit risk, but not for liquidity

- **Policy approval:** Data governance policy RDG-01 v2.3 was approved by the CRO in March 2025. The Management Board is informed of material changes once a year.
- **Data owners:** Owners are appointed for retail and corporate credit risk data. Owners for liquidity and market risk data are still to be determined.
- **Data quality committee:** The policy names the CRO as chair in section 4.3 and the CDO as chair in section 6.4. In practice both claim the role.
- **Subsidiaries:** Bright Bank Deutschland and FBB Lease are outside the policy's scope and deliver their figures in their own formats.

*Source: FBB policy RDG-01 v2.3; stakeholder interviews*

## Slide 11: 37 spreadsheet tools sit in the reporting chain and must be removed

**37** end-user computing tools in the credit and liquidity risk reporting chain

- BCBS 239 Principle 3 requires risk data aggregation to be fully automated
- Manual processes are therefore not permitted
- Only part of the 37 tools is inventoried today
- All 37 tools must be decommissioned by year-end 2027

*Source: BCBS 239 (2013), Principle 3; stakeholder interviews*

## Slide 12: Internal risk reporting is too slow for timely decisions

**26** working days to deliver the monthly group risk report
**20** working days: beyond this the ECB generally considers a monthly report too slow

- Policy RDG-01 allows 30 working days
- Reporting frequency stays the same in stress
- Ad hoc requests take up to ten working days

*Source: Stakeholder interviews; FBB policy RDG-01 sections 9.2–9.4; ECB guide on RDARR (2024), section 3.6*

## Slide 13: Bright Bank Deutschland sits outside the data governance framework

**~12%** of group deposits are held by Bright Bank Deutschland

- The subsidiary delivers risk data in separate spreadsheets
- Liquidity risk data is governed by Treasury's own procedures
- The ECB expects the framework to cover all material legal entities and risks

*Source: FBB policy RDG-01 section 2; stakeholder interviews; ECB guide on RDARR (2024), section 3.2*

| 2 Data architecture | Gap | Definitions per department; no group dictionary |
| 3 Accuracy and integrity | Partial | No reconciliation with accounting data |

| 6 Adaptability | Partial | Ad hoc requests take up to ten working days |
| 7 Accuracy (reporting) | Partial | Manual adjustments in spreadsheets |
| 8 Comprehensiveness | Met | All material risk types in the board report |
| 9 Clarity and usefulness | Met | Board report rated clear in interviews |

*Source: Team assessment against BCBS 239 (2013). Principles 12–14 apply to supervisors.*

## Slide 15: Six recommendations close the main gaps

1. Management Board approves the framework and appoints an accountable member
2. Bring liquidity, market risk and both subsidiaries into scope
3. Transfer ownership of liquidity risk data to Group IT, which runs the LCR systems
4. Build a group-wide data dictionary and attribute-level lineage
5. Cut monthly risk report production to 15 working days or less
6. Set up independent validation and an internal audit review

*Source: Team analysis; ECB guide on RDARR (2024), sections 3.1–3.6*

## Slide 17: Generative AI can transform risk management at FBB

- **Automated risk narratives:** Large language models draft the commentary for the monthly risk report, cutting production time and freeing analysts for higher-value work.
- **Early-warning signals:** Machine learning on payment and transaction data detects deteriorating SME borrowers months before arrears appear.
- **Regulatory change radar:** An AI agent tracks new supervisory publications and maps them to FBB's policies automatically.

*Source: Team analysis; industry publications*

## Slide 18: A cloud data platform is the foundation for the future

- **Phase 1 · 2027:** Select a strategic cloud provider and migrate the risk data warehouse to a modern lakehouse architecture.
- **Phase 2 · 2028:** Move finance and treasury data to the same platform, creating one source of truth for the whole bank.
- **Phase 3 · 2029:** Decommission legacy systems and on-premise data centres, with expected run-cost savings.

*Source: Team analysis*

## Slide 19: ESG data will be the next supervisory focus

- **Climate risk data:** Collect energy labels and physical risk data for the full mortgage book to support climate stress testing.
- **CSRD reporting:** Bring sustainability disclosures under the same data governance as risk and finance data.
- **One ESG data owner:** Appoint a single group owner for sustainability data, reporting to the CDO.

*Source: Team analysis; industry publications*
